Upper Contour Sets
& Supporting Hyperplanes

Build the geometry one layer at a time. Move x* to explore how a supergradient supports an upper contour set.

Cf(x*) = {x : f(x) ≥ f(x*)}

H = {x : p · (x − x*) = 0}

H+ = {x : p · (x − x*) ≥ 0}

Cf(x*) ⊆ H+. Lavender lies within the supporting halfspace.

Values rounded to 2 decimals.

Why does the halfspace contain the upper contour set?

Both functions are concave. A supergradient p satisfies f(x) ≤ f(x*) + p · (x − x*). So f(x) ≥ f(x*) implies p · (x − x*) ≥ 0.

Here H is a line in the (x₁, x₂) plane supporting Cf(x*). The graph of f would live in three dimensions.

Quadratic: p = (1, −2x₂*).
